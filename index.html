🌊 Ocean Science

Waves, Oceans & Our Planet

The ocean covers 71% of Earth's surface and its waves are powered by wind, gravity, and energy transfer. Understanding ocean waves helps us protect coastal communities and harness renewable energy.

🌊
🌊

Ocean Waves

How do waves carry energy across thousands of kilometres of ocean?

Tap to explore →

Ocean Wave Science

Ocean waves are formed by wind transferring energy to the water surface. The water molecules move in circles — they don't travel with the wave! Only the energy moves forward. A wave's speed depends on water depth: v = √(g × d) in shallow water.

🌍 Tsunamis can travel at 800 km/h in deep ocean — as fast as a jet plane!
🚨

Tsunami Detection

How do scientists use wave technology to warn communities in time?

Tap to explore →

Early Warning Systems

Seismographs detect the seismic waves from undersea earthquakes. Ocean buoys measure sudden changes in sea level caused by tsunami waves. This data is sent via satellite radio waves to warning centres, triggering alerts within minutes.

⚡ The 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami killed 230,000 people. Modern detection systems can now warn communities in under 3 minutes!
⚡

Wave Energy

Can we use ocean waves to generate clean electricity?

Tap to explore →

Wave Power Converters

Wave energy converters (WECs) capture the up-and-down motion of ocean waves. A buoy rises and falls with each wave, driving a generator. The power output depends on wave amplitude and frequency: larger, more frequent waves = more energy!

🔋 Wave energy could provide up to 10% of global electricity needs if fully harnessed!
🐳

Marine Ecosystems

How do waves and light affect life in the ocean?

Tap to explore →

Light & Life Underwater

Sunlight (visible EM waves) only penetrates the top 200 m of the ocean — the "photic zone." This is where 90% of marine life exists. Coral reefs depend on light for photosynthesis. Sound waves also play a role: whales communicate using low-frequency waves that travel thousands of kilometres!

🐋 Blue whales' calls at 10–40 Hz can be heard 1,600 km away underwater!

🌱 Sustainable Science

Wave Technology for a Greener Future

Wave and light technologies are at the heart of sustainable development. From solar panels to smart cities, electromagnetic waves are helping humans reduce their environmental footprint.

🌱
☀️

Solar Energy

How do solar panels convert light waves directly into electricity?

Tap to explore →

The Photovoltaic Effect

Solar panels use the photoelectric effect: photons (light particles) from sunlight hit silicon atoms and knock electrons loose, creating an electric current. Higher frequency (blue) light carries more energy per photon than lower frequency (red) light.

☀️ Solar energy is growing 25% per year. One hour of sunlight could power the world for a year!
📡

Smart Technology

How do EM waves make cities more efficient and sustainable?

Tap to explore →

IoT & Smart Grids

Smart cities use microwave and radio wave networks (IoT sensors) to monitor energy use, traffic, and pollution in real time. Smart grids use these signals to balance electricity supply and demand, reducing waste by up to 30%. Less energy waste = less CO₂ emissions!

🏙️ Smart city technology could reduce energy consumption by 30% and CO₂ emissions by 15%!
🌍

Environmental Monitoring

How do scientists use waves to track climate change from space?

Tap to explore →

Satellites & Remote Sensing

Earth observation satellites use microwave and infrared waves to measure sea surface temperature, ice coverage, deforestation, and air pollution. The data is sent back via radio waves, helping scientists track climate change with incredible precision.

🛰️ NASA's satellites have tracked a 4mm/year rise in sea levels since 1993 using microwave altimeters!
🌬️

Renewable Energy

Beyond solar — how else are waves used in clean energy?

Tap to explore →

Wind + Wave Synergy

Wind turbines convert mechanical wave energy (air pressure waves) into electricity. Offshore turbines pair well with wave energy converters. Infrared sensors monitor turbine health remotely. The combination of solar, wind, and wave energy could supply all global electricity needs!

⚡ Indonesia has 17,000+ islands — wave and solar energy could power remote communities without fossil fuels!
